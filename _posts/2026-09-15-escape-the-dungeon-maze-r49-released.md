The September 2026 update <span style="background-color: #4CAF50; color: white; padding: 3px 8px; border-radius: 12px; font-size: 0.85em; margin-left: 5px;">R4.9 - September 2026</span> for "The Dungeon - Get Out" and the corresponding Level Editor is now **officially released**!

This one is less about new things to find in the maze and more about where the maze itself comes from. For years the Level Editor has been a separate companion app: build a dungeon there, export it, import it into the game, go and play. As of this release that round trip is gone — the **editor lives inside the game**. Along the way the editor got a completely new interface, and the game learned two new languages.

## The Level Editor Moves Into the Game
![The game's main menu on iPhone with the new 'Buy the Level Editor' entry between Settings & Controls and Info](/assets/2026/09/Editor0.png)

The main menu has a new entry. Tap it and the full Level Editor opens right there in the game — the same level pack list, the same map editor, the same tile, monster and item tools you know from the standalone app. Design a pack with several levels, place monsters, items, doors, switches, traps and triggers, then step straight into your creation and play it, without ever leaving the app. Once you own it, the button simply becomes **Level Editor** and takes you there directly.

Level packs still travel as `.etdm` files, so sharing works exactly as before: export a pack from the built-in editor and hand it to a friend, or import one that somebody else made.

## One Purchase, No Subscription

![The in-game purchase screen for the Level Editor: a preview of the iPad editor, a feature list and a one-time 'Buy' button](/assets/2026/09/Editor1.png)

The built-in editor is unlocked with a single **in-app purchase** — a one-time buy, no subscription, restorable on every device signed in with the same Apple account. The purchase screen shows what you get: draw mazes tile by tile or let the generator do it, place monsters, items, doors, switches and traps, build puzzles with triggers and actions, and share your level packs with other players.

The game itself stays free to download and play, as it always has been. The purchase is only for those who want to build.

## A Completely Reworked Editor

![The reworked Level Editor on iPad: the level map on the left, the monster inspector with position, behavior and skill level on the right](/assets/2026/09/Editor2.png)

Building the editor into the game meant rebuilding it, and that is what this release delivers on both sides. Every screen of the Level Editor — the start page, the level pack overview, the level editor itself, the tile and trigger inspectors, the monster and item editors — has been **rebuilt from the ground up**. The map sits on the left, a sidebar with the tools on the right, and the inspectors switch between settings, textures, monsters and items with a single tap. Pickers are sheets, edits apply immediately, and the whole thing finally feels like one app rather than a stack of forms.

The **standalone Level Editor app** ships this same reworked interface and has exactly the same features as the built-in version. I will keep maintaining it for the near future, so nothing you have built there is going anywhere. That said, if you are starting fresh, I recommend unlocking the editor inside the game instead — editing and playing your dungeons in one place is simply the better integrated experience, and it is where the editor's future lies.

## Spanish and French

The game now speaks **Spanish** and **French** in addition to English and German. Menus, dialogs, item and spell names, the tutorial and the in-game texts are all translated, and the App Store listing follows in all four languages. If your device is set to one of them, the game picks it up automatically.

And as usual, both the game and the editor come with a round of **stability improvements and bug fixes** — the kind you should not notice, because things simply keep working.
